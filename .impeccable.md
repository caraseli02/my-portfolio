# Impeccable Design Context

## Users
Recruiters and hiring managers evaluating a Vue.js / frontend specialist. They scan fast, often on laptops, comparing dozens of portfolios. Their context: mid-task, time-constrained, looking for signals of craft and competence. The portfolio must hook them in under 10 seconds.

## Brand Personality
Technical, dense, impressive. The interface should feel like opening a well-engineered tool — every pixel intentional, every interaction deliberate. Not flashy for its own sake, but the kind of dense that signals deep expertise. Think: the person who reads source code for fun.

## Aesthetic Direction
Terminal-meets-control-panel: monospace-heavy type layered over a structured grid, with the density and precision of a well-configured IDE. Light mode is warm parchment with sharp typographic contrast. Dark mode is deep charcoal with syntax-highlighted accents. Both modes feel like working environments, not marketing pages.

Key references: Bloomberg terminal density, Hacker News utilitarianism, VS Code's dark theme precision, the restraint of a well-set man page.

Anti-references: generic dev portfolios with gradient heroes, corporate SaaS templates, artsy layouts that sacrifice readability, anything that looks like it was generated by a template builder.

## Design Principles

1. **Density signals competence.** Recruiters should see depth at a glance — more information, tighter spacing, richer detail than expected. Not cluttered, but packed with intent.

2. **Interactive proof over claims.** Live code snippets, working demos, and interactive elements replace bullet-point skill lists. Show, don't tell.

3. **Technical authenticity.** Use the visual language of development tools — monospace type, syntax highlighting, terminal prompts, code-block aesthetics — not as decoration but as honest expression of craft.

4. **Dual-theme as craft signal.** Light and dark modes both fully realized, with a seamless toggle. The mode switch itself is a showcase of frontend skill.

5. **Fast to parse, slow to leave.** Recruiters scan in 10 seconds — hook them with visual density and interactive elements. Then reward deeper inspection with hidden details and micro-interactions.
